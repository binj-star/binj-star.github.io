Article 01

Why do college students often neglect healthy eating?

Many students have irregular schedules, frequently missing regular meal times due to assignments, classes, or socializing. In such a fast-paced environment...

Many students have irregular schedules, frequently missing regular meal times due to assignments, classes, or socializing. In such a fast-paced environment, convenience and cost often take precedence over nutrition, which is a major reason why healthy eating is hard to sustain.

When it's 11 PM and you are stressed in the library, a quick slice of pizza or a bag of chips feels like the only viable option. We often fall into the trap of thinking that eating healthy requires hours of meal prep and an expensive grocery budget.

However, healthy eating doesn't have to be complicated or expensive. For college students, the most realistic approach is to start with small changes—such as ensuring at least one balanced meal a day, reducing sugary drinks, or keeping healthier snacks in the dorm. It is about building a sustainable system, not punishing yourself with strict diets.

Article 02

Post-interview Observations: It's not just about "eating light"

During the interviews, I found that students have varying definitions of "healthy eating." Some believe less oil and salt is the standard; some care more about feeling full...

During the interviews, I found that students have varying definitions of "healthy eating." Some believe less oil and salt is the standard; some care more about feeling full and getting enough protein, while others are primarily concerned about whether it fits their tight budget.

One striking observation was that many students feel a sense of guilt when they can't afford trendy "superfoods" like avocados or organic quinoa, leading them to abandon the idea of healthy eating altogether.

These diverse responses show that healthy eating for students is deeply intertwined with time, financial status, and lifestyle. Therefore, when discussing this topic, we shouldn't just offer theoretical "standard answers," but also consider the students' real living environments. Real nutrition must be accessible and affordable.

Article 03

How does our environment shape our food choices?

Often, we eat snacks not because we are truly hungry, but because they are too easily accessible. If you leave a bag of chips on your desk, you will likely eat it mindlessly...

Often, we eat snacks not because we are truly hungry, but because they are too easily accessible. If you leave a bag of chips on your desk, you will likely eat it mindlessly while studying.

Behavioral economics calls this the "nudge" theory. Humans are biologically wired to seek high-calorie foods with the least amount of effort. If junk food is in your immediate line of sight, your willpower will eventually lose the battle.

By adjusting our physical environment—such as placing fresh fruit on the desk and hiding ultra-processed foods deep in a zipped bag under the bed—we can use this slight "friction" to significantly help us build better habits effortlessly. You don't need more discipline; you just need a better environment.

Article 04

A Win-Win Strategy for Health and the Environment

When we choose natural plant-based ingredients, we not only provide sustained energy for our brains but also drastically reduce plastic packaging waste and carbon emissions...

When we choose natural plant-based ingredients (like oats, beans, lentils, and fresh produce), we not only provide sustained energy for our brains but also drastically reduce plastic packaging waste and carbon emissions.

Look at the typical €5 supermarket "meal deal": a highly processed sandwich wrapped in thick plastic, a plastic bottle of soda, and a bag of crisps. The environmental cost of this convenience is massive.

Buying one less over-packaged fast-food item is actually a smart choice that is responsible for both our bodies and the Earth. The food that is best for your brain focus happens to be the exact same food that is best for the planet.

Article 05

The "Smart Swap" Rule for University Students

To eat healthier, you don't need to quit all your favorite snacks like a monk. Learning to make "Smart Swaps" is the key to long-term success...

To eat healthier, you don't need to quit all your favorite snacks like a monk. Learning to make "Smart Swaps" is the key to long-term success.

Many diets fail because they rely on deprivation. Instead of saying "I can never eat sugar again," ask yourself, "What is a slightly better alternative?"

For example, replace an expensive €3 high-sugar energy drink with warm lemon water, or swap late-night crisps for a crunchy apple with peanut butter. You still get the crunch and the energy, but these tiny adjustments are cheaper, fresher, and highly effective at preventing the dreaded 3 PM energy crash.

About This Project

This website is a digital platform I built to promote and advocate for healthy eating among university students.

Project Intent

In the fast-paced college life, many students often resort to convenient but unhealthy ultra-processed foods due to academic stress and budget constraints. The original intent of this project is to break the myth that "healthy eating is expensive and troublesome", helping everyone take care of their brains and bodies in the simplest ways.

Core Vision

I hope to provide a stress-free space by sharing practical dietary advice, real interview stories, and easy-to-follow concepts. Change doesn't happen overnight; by starting with drinking an extra glass of water a day or making one smart snack swap, we can all build our own sustainable and healthy lifestyles.
